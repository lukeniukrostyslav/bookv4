# US OPPORTUNITY GAP ANALYSIS — BOOKV4

Date: 2026-09-21

## Objective

Identify commercially interesting reader/problem spaces in the U.S. market without claiming a guaranteed bestseller outcome.

## Current evidence

Pew's 2025 U.S. survey reports 31% of adults read an e-book in the prior 12 months; 41% among ages 18–29 and 33% among ages 30–49. Fourteen percent of U.S. adults read more than 20 books in the year. These are readership indicators, not purchase-frequency measurements.

Circana reports strong 2026 adult-fiction performance across science fiction, thrillers and romance. Its 2025 U.S. romance analysis reported 24% year-to-date print growth, with romantasy and sports romance among the fastest-growing romance subjects, while psychological thrillers and dark fantasy also showed growth.

Circana's 2026 market analysis also reports consumer interest in story-driven nonfiction including true crime and in escapist/speculative fiction including science fiction and horror.

Publishers Weekly's summary of Circana BookScan reports that U.S. print romance sales rose 3.9% in 2025 to almost 44 million units, while fantasy print sales fell 8.7% to 24.1 million. This is print data, not Kindle sales.

## Opportunity map

### Lane A — Dark romance / romantasy
**Demand signal:** strong.
**Buyer promise:** intense emotional stakes + escapism + romance.
**Competition:** very high.
**Differentiation requirement:** very high.
**Series potential:** high.
**Translation feasibility:** high if cultural references remain adaptable.
**Research status:** promising but crowded.

### Lane B — Psychological / domestic thriller
**Demand signal:** strong.
**Buyer promise:** suspense, secrets, reversals, fast emotional payoff.
**Competition:** very high.
**Differentiation requirement:** very high.
**Series potential:** medium; standalone is common.
**Translation feasibility:** high.
**Research status:** promising; requires a highly distinctive central hook.

### Lane C — Mystery / suspense with a specific everyday hook
**Demand signal:** established.
**Buyer promise:** puzzle + revelation + accessible setting.
**Competition:** high.
**Differentiation requirement:** high.
**Series potential:** high.
**Translation feasibility:** high.
**Research status:** worth deeper testing.

### Lane D — Speculative / dark fantasy
**Demand signal:** current escapist/speculative strength.
**Buyer promise:** immersive world + danger + transformation.
**Competition:** high.
**Differentiation requirement:** high.
**Series potential:** very high.
**Production complexity:** high.
**Translation feasibility:** medium-high.
**Research status:** promising but expensive to execute well.

### Lane E — Story-driven nonfiction / true crime
**Demand signal:** current U.S. interest.
**Buyer promise:** real-world mystery + emotional narrative + explanation.
**Competition:** high.
**Differentiation requirement:** high.
**Production risk:** factual/legal verification is substantial.
**Translation feasibility:** medium; U.S.-specific cases may not transfer as well internationally.
**Research status:** commercially interesting but higher factual-risk burden.

### Lane F — Self-help / psychology
**Demand signal:** large established audience, but 2026 self-help print sales were weak in Q1 according to Circana/Publishers Weekly.
**Buyer promise:** solve a specific problem.
**Competition:** very high.
**Differentiation requirement:** extremely high.
**Credibility burden:** high.
**Translation feasibility:** high for universal problems.
**Research status:** do not select merely because the category is large.

## Opportunity-gap hypotheses

These are hypotheses, not validated market facts:

1. A genre-mashup with a very simple one-sentence promise may be easier to market than a generic genre label.
2. A thriller/mystery built around an immediately recognizable everyday problem could offer a clearer hook than a generic murder mystery.
3. A darker romance/fantasy concept may have discovery potential but needs unusually specific differentiation because competition is intense.
4. A speculative story with a strong emotional premise and limited worldbuilding complexity could reduce production burden versus a giant epic fantasy.
5. A practical nonfiction book should solve one sharply defined problem rather than cover a broad self-improvement category.

## Current research decision

Do not lock the genre yet.

The strongest areas for the next title-level test are:
- dark romance / romantasy;
- psychological/domestic thriller;
- specific-hook mystery;
- speculative/dark fantasy.

Story-driven nonfiction remains a secondary test lane because of factual and legal workload.

## Next gate: buyer-intent validation

For each of the four primary lanes, collect a larger title sample and evidence about:
- current bestseller visibility;
- review volume;
- price;
- series behavior;
- reader complaints;
- repeated hooks;
- unmet reader expectations;
- social discovery;
- search/category positioning.

Only then create P6 concepts.
