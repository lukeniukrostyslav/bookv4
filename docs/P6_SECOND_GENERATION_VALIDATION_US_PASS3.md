# P6 SECOND-GENERATION VALIDATION — US MARKET PASS 3

Date: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Validate the eight second-generation concepts against current U.S. market signals and comparable mechanisms before any move to P7.

This is a commercial screening document, not a claim of sales success or bestseller potential.

## Market context

Current U.S. Kindle category estimates indicate substantial activity in women-centered mystery/thriller/suspense. Kindleranker currently reports a median monthly sales figure of $46,085 for the top 30 books in Women's Fiction Mystery, Thriller & Suspense and $29,854 for Women Sleuths; these are third-party estimates and should be treated as directional rather than official Amazon sales data. A separate August 2026 K-lytics-based report estimated nearly 13,000 daily sales/borrows across a filtered non-cozy female-detective segment. These figures support demand but also indicate substantial competition. 

Publishers Weekly's Fall 2026 mystery/thriller preview shows continued use of missing-person, false-identity, family-secret, and technology/identity hooks. Current September 2026 review coverage also includes mysteries built around missing people and competing investigative perspectives. Therefore the project should favor a mechanism that creates a distinct reader question rather than another generic disappearance or unreliable-narrator story.

## Concept-by-concept validation

### Concept 9 — THE RECORD SAYS SHE NEVER LEFT

**Status: ADVANCE TO DEEP VALIDATION**

Why it works:
- Familiar promise: missing sister / investigation.
- Distinct mechanism: official records and physical evidence create a direct contradiction.
- Grounded route is possible: building access logs, badge systems, CCTV, phone location, transportation records and human records can disagree without supernatural rules.
- Strong chapter engine: each verified record can contradict another.

Overlap risks:
- Security footage and identity falsification are familiar thriller devices.
- Current fiction already uses records/identity erasure and security footage contradictions.

What must be unique:
- The contradiction should emerge from ordinary systems rather than a secret omnipotent hacker.
- The reader must be able to understand the evidence chain and solve parts of it.

Commercial assessment: **strongest grounded candidate so far**.

### Concept 10 — THE WOMAN WHO REMEMBERED TOMORROW

**Status: REJECT AS CURRENT CONCEPT; DO NOT BUILD**

Reason:
- Exact title already exists as a 2026 independently published psychological thriller by Atlee Pa.
- Additional online fiction uses the same future-memory premise.
- The mechanism therefore fails the originality gate before deeper development.

Evidence: the 2026 Atlee Pa book is listed by multiple booksellers with the exact title and psychological-thriller positioning; another 2026 online work uses essentially the same "remembering tomorrow" premise. 

Action: archive the concept. Do not reuse its title or central mechanism.

### Concept 11 — THE SECOND VERSION OF THE TRUTH

**Status: HOLD / REWORK**

Strength:
- Multi-witness contradiction gives a clear puzzle.
- No missing-person dependency.
- Physical evidence can make the mystery fair-play.

Overlap:
- Multiple versions of one event are a well-established psychological-fiction mechanism.
- Current reader discussion and recent fiction demonstrate the pattern of different protagonists carrying different versions of the same event.

Problem:
- Without a stronger external mechanism, it risks becoming a conventional unreliable-memory / Rashomon-style thriller.

Required rework:
- Replace "memory may be wrong" with a concrete information mechanism that causes the divergence.

### Concept 12 — THE DAY THE EVIDENCE CHANGED

**Status: HOLD / HIGH EXECUTION RISK**

Strength:
- Very clear one-line mystery.
- Evidence itself becomes the antagonist/puzzle.

Risk:
- Literal changing photographs immediately suggests supernatural or science-fiction rules.
- A grounded explanation risks feeling like a technical trick unless carefully designed.
- Security-camera/photo manipulation is already familiar in thriller fiction.

Required rework:
- Make the apparent "change" a consequence of how evidence is processed, stored, authenticated or interpreted, while preserving fair-play clues.

### Concept 13 — THE TEN-MINUTE WINDOW

**Status: HOLD / REWORK**

Strength:
- The ten-minute rule creates a repeatable countdown.
- Strong structural potential for short chapters and cliffhangers.

Overlap:
- Exact time-window suspense is a common narrative device; a current 2026 work is already titled *The Ten-Minute Window*, although its premise differs.

Problem:
- The municipal archive + dead mother's credentials combination needs a highly credible mechanism.
- The current title should not be used because it is already in circulation.

Required rework:
- Change title and make the window an unavoidable real-world constraint rather than a mysterious magical access rule.

### Concept 14 — THE PERSON WHO WAS NEVER REPORTED MISSING

**Status: REJECT AS CURRENT CONCEPT; DO NOT BUILD**

Reason:
- The core hook is already directly represented by a September 2026 independently published mystery, *The Collector's Room*, whose premise explicitly centers on women who were never reported missing.
- A separate 2026 thriller is titled *The Girl Who Was Never Reported Missing* and uses the same central phrase and missing-person premise.
- Older mystery fiction also uses the device of a dead or unidentified person who was never reported missing.

Action: archive this concept. Do not attempt to win on the same central hook.

### Concept 15 — THE CALL THAT ONLY SHE RECEIVED

**Status: ADVANCE TO DEEP VALIDATION, WITH REWRITE**

Strength:
- Same emergency event + different messages is a strong information-asymmetry engine.
- Family members having incompatible instructions creates immediate interpersonal tension.
- Can remain grounded through call routing, edited recordings, selective forwarding, or staged communication.

Risk:
- "Different message to each person" is not enough by itself; the mechanism must be credible.

Required rewrite:
- Define exactly why each recipient receives a different message and why the sender cannot simply contact everyone directly.

Commercial assessment: **strong second candidate if the mechanism is made realistic and the central event is highly compelling**.

### Concept 16 — THE FILE WITH HER NAME ON IT

**Status: HOLD / REWORK**

Strength:
- Immediate curiosity.
- Strong identity mystery.

Overlap:
- Identity-erasure and impossible-record premises are already present in current speculative/thriller fiction.
- The exact "file with her name" device is common enough that the file itself cannot be the differentiator.

Risk:
- High plausibility burden if the protagonist's current address and photograph appear in a genuinely old file.
- Easy drift into clone/time-travel/conspiracy territory.

Required rework:
- Ground the archive mechanism in a believable institutional process and make the apparent impossibility arise from a specific document trail.
