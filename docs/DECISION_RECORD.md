## Evidence reviewed

- U.S. adult eBook readership: Pew Research Center reports 31% of U.S. adults read an e-book in the prior 12 months in 2025; this is a readership measure, not a purchase measure.
- Amazon KDP documentation emphasizes accurate category selection and researching genre/category fit.
- Pubscout analyzed 34,700+ U.S. Kindle listings and provides listing-level signals for Romance, Fantasy, Self-Help, Mystery and Thriller.
- Additional third-party 2026 market analyses were reviewed as directional evidence only.

## Current candidate lanes

1. Romance / defined romance subgenre
2. Mystery / thriller / suspense
3. Fantasy / romantasy
4. Self-help / popular psychology

## Why no concept is locked yet

The available evidence is sufficient to establish commercially relevant lanes, but not sufficient to select one book concept responsibly. Purchase intent, concrete competitor-title patterns, differentiation space, production burden, and international potential still need a title-level/subgenre-level comparison.

## Next gate

Build a competitor-title matrix using concrete recent Kindle titles and comparable subgenres. Record:
- title / author
- subgenre
- approximate positioning
- price where available
- review signal where available
- cover convention
- blurb promise
- recurring trope/problem
- differentiation opportunities
- observed weaknesses/reader complaints where reliable evidence exists

Only after this gate should a commercial concept be selected.

## Anti-bias rule

Do not select a genre merely because it is large, fashionable, or associated with high third-party revenue estimates. The final concept must be justified by multiple evidence dimensions.
