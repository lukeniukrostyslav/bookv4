# US BUYER-INTENT VALIDATION — BOOKV4

Date: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Move from broad market size toward observable signals of what U.S. readers are actively choosing in current bestseller ecosystems.

## Current evidence

### Format audience

Pew's October 2025 U.S. survey found 31% of adults had read an e-book in the prior 12 months. The rate was 41% for ages 18–29 and 33% for ages 30–49. Fourteen percent of adults read more than 20 books in the year. These are reading measures, not purchase measures.

### Adult fiction

Circana reports adult genre fiction has dominated 2026 bestseller performance, with particular strength in science fiction, thrillers and romance.

Circana also reports that adult fiction volume in 2025 was nearly 50% above 2020 levels, despite only 1% year-over-year growth in 2025. This indicates a larger post-pandemic baseline rather than explosive current growth across all fiction.

### Romance

Circana reports U.S. print romance sales were up 24% year-to-date in its 2025 analysis, with romantasy and sports romance among the fastest-growing romance subjects. Circana's Future of Books study also found 26% of romance buyers said they were reading much more than a year earlier.

A monthly analysis of the U.S. paid Kindle Romance Top 100 in June 2026 showed a mix of contemporary romance, sports romance, romantasy/dark romance and established backlist titles. This is a third-party ranking analysis, not official Amazon sales data.

### Thriller / mystery

Current U.S. bestseller reporting shows repeated presence of thriller, mystery and suspense titles. A third-party August 2026 Klytics analysis, reported by industry media, estimated mystery/thriller/suspense represented 31% of the U.S. Kindle Top 100, versus 48% for romance including romantasy. Because this is an external estimate and methodology is not independently verified here, treat the percentages as directional.

A separate September 2026 third-party Klytics report estimated strong Kindle activity for non-cozy female-detective fiction. This is also an estimate, not an official Amazon unit-sales report.

## Buyer-intent signals

### Signal 1 — High-frequency readers matter

The 14% reading 20+ books/year are an attractive potential repeat-purchase segment. We should design for a reader who can finish one book and want another, rather than a one-off information buyer.

### Signal 2 — Genre promise must be immediate

Current bestseller ecosystems show recognizable promises: romance/relationship outcome, suspense/mystery/revelation, or immersive speculative escape. A new title should communicate its primary emotional/reading payoff immediately.

### Signal 3 — Series/backlist can amplify demand

Current Kindle bestseller lists contain many series entries and established backlist titles. This suggests a repeatable concept can have more commercial runway than a book with no credible follow-on.

### Signal 4 — Competition is real

High visibility does not mean low entry difficulty. Popular lanes contain established authors and large backlists. The concept therefore needs a differentiated hook rather than generic genre compliance.

## Provisional audience profiles to test

### A — High-frequency romance/romantasy reader
Likely value: emotional intensity, chemistry, escapism, series continuation.

### B — High-frequency thriller/mystery reader
Likely value: immediate premise, secrets, reversals, short chapters/pacing, satisfying revelation.

### C — Speculative escape reader
Likely value: distinctive premise, immersive setting, progression, danger and series potential.

### D — Female-led mystery/crime reader
Likely value: competent protagonist, investigation, danger, psychological tension, satisfying resolution.

These are working hypotheses, not inferred facts about any individual.
