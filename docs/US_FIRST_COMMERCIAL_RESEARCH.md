# US-FIRST COMMERCIAL RESEARCH PLAN — BOOKV4

Date: 2026-09-21

## Strategic correction

BOOKV4's primary commercial market is now the United States.

The creative manuscript may be written first in Russian for production efficiency, but the commercial concept must be selected with the future U.S. English edition in mind.

Italy is a secondary localization market, not the primary market-selection driver.

## Why US-first

Pew Research Center's 2025 U.S. survey reports:
- 31% of U.S. adults read an e-book in the prior 12 months;
- 41% among ages 18–29;
- 33% among ages 30–49;
- 14% of U.S. adults read more than 20 books in the prior year.

These are readership measures, not direct purchase measures, so they establish addressable reading behavior rather than guaranteed buyers.

Circana's 2026 U.S. book-market analysis identifies current growth pockets including story-driven nonfiction, true crime, science fiction and horror, while noting pressure on discretionary book spending.

Amazon KDP states that authors should research genres and balance category popularity with relevance, and that the primary marketplace should guide category selection.

## Research priority

1. United States buyer/readership evidence — primary.
2. English-language competitive titles — primary.
3. U.S. subgenre/category structure — primary.
4. Reader pain points and purchase triggers — primary.
5. Series and repeat-purchase behavior — primary.
6. International portability — secondary.
7. Italy — secondary localization signal.

## Candidate commercial lanes

Do not lock any lane yet:
- Romance / romantasy
- Psychological / domestic thriller
- Mystery / suspense
- Fantasy / dark fantasy
- Self-help / psychology
- Story-driven nonfiction / true crime

## Required evidence before P6

For each lane, build a U.S.-focused matrix covering:
- concrete comparable titles;
- publication recency;
- price/format;
- review-count signal;
- series status;
- recurring tropes or reader promise;
- cover conventions;
- blurb conventions;
- reader complaints;
- unmet needs;
- social/discovery signals;
- translation/adaptation feasibility;
- production complexity.

## Commercial principle

The goal is not to claim or promise a bestseller.

The goal is to maximize the probability of commercial traction by selecting a concept where:
**large enough buyer audience + strong purchase intent + clear reader promise + differentiated hook + manageable competition + repeat/series potential + international portability**

all overlap.

No single metric will determine the concept.
