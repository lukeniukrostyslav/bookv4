## Candidate stress test

### Concept 1 — The House That Remembers
**Status: HOLD / needs stronger differentiation.**
- Fits psychological-thriller demand.
- Memory/reality mechanism is attractive but the haunted-house/memory territory is crowded.
- Needs a sharper commercial promise and a distinctive mechanism.
- Good English portability.
- Not ready for approval.

### Concept 2 — The Woman in the Family Photo
**Status: STRONG VALIDATION CANDIDATE.**
- Immediate visual hook.
- Easy to communicate in title, cover and blurb.
- Strong fit with psychological/domestic mystery.
- Standalone structure is feasible.
- Main risk: the reveal must be genuinely fresh rather than a familiar identity/twin twist.
- Good English-market portability.

### Concept 3 — The Man I Was Supposed to Hate
**Status: HOLD.**
- Romance + suspense aligns with active market lanes.
- Dark-romance competition is particularly strong.
- The premise currently resembles familiar revenge/enemy-romance structures.
- Needs a much more distinctive central conflict before approval.

### Concept 4 — The Last Voicemail
**Status: STRONG VALIDATION CANDIDATE.**
- Extremely clear hook.
- Easy cover/blurb communication.
- Mystery mechanism naturally creates chapter-level suspense.
- Good standalone structure with possible anthology/series expansion.
- Main risk: repeated-message mechanics can become predictable.
- Excellent English portability.

### Concept 5 — Tomorrow's Missing Person
**Status: STRONG VALIDATION CANDIDATE.**
- High-concept premise with immediate stakes.
- Speculative + thriller crossover fits current escapist/speculative demand.
- Strong series possibility.
- Main risk: the prediction mechanism must have internally consistent rules.
- Very high English-market portability.

### Concept 6 — The Witch Who Could Steal a Memory
**Status: HOLD / high execution risk.**
- Strong fantasy-romance promise and series potential.
- Romantasy currently has strong commercial visibility. citeturn0search4turn0search8
- Competition and production burden are high.
- Memory-based romance mechanism is promising but needs deeper differentiation.
- Not ready for approval.

### Concept 7 — The Neighbor Who Doesn't Exist
**Status: STRONG VALIDATION CANDIDATE.**
- Very short, visual, high-curiosity premise.
- Strong domestic psychological positioning.
- Cover/blurb potential is high.
- Main risk: premise overlaps with reality-distortion and unreliable-narrator conventions.
- Requires a fresh explanation for the apparent contradiction.

### Concept 8 — The Psychology of the Perfect Lie
**Status: SECONDARY.**
- Story-driven nonfiction has current U.S. interest, including true crime and emotionally engaging nonfiction. citeturn0search5
- High fact-checking and sourcing burden.
- Less clean fit with the fiction-market opportunity discovered in this research.
- Could become a separate nonfiction project later.

## Current shortlist for deeper validation

Without declaring a winner, the concepts with the clearest next-stage validation case are:

1. **The Woman in the Family Photo**
2. **The Last Voicemail**
3. **Tomorrow's Missing Person**
4. **The Neighbor Who Doesn't Exist**

The reason is not that they are "best"; they currently have the clearest combination of immediate hook, simple marketing explanation, English portability and manageable production compared with the higher-complexity fantasy/romance candidates.

## Next validation gate

For these four candidates, research:
- 10+ close U.S. comparable titles per concept;
- exact premise overlap;
- publication recency;
- series/standalone behavior;
- cover language;
- blurb promise;
- review-volume signals;
- recurring negative reader feedback;
- identifiable unmet expectations;
- potential title/cover differentiation;
- possible series expansion.

Only after this deeper comparison should one concept be approved for P7 architecture.
