# P7 STORY ENGINE — EVIDENCE TIMELINE, SUSPECT MAP, CLUES AND REVEAL

Date: 2026-09-21
Market: United States first
Working title: The Record Says She Never Left

## 1. Locked story engine

Nora Bennett investigates her sister Claire's disappearance. The official record says Claire left her apartment building at 8:17 p.m. and continued through a sequence of ordinary locations. Physical evidence proves that at least one part of that sequence could not have happened as recorded.

The false timeline is not created by one impossible technology. It is built through a chain of legitimate systems that inherit, repeat and reinforce an initial false assumption.

The central mystery is therefore:

**Who created the first false fact, why was it created, and how did ordinary systems turn that false fact into an apparently undeniable history?**

## 2. Definitive antagonist objective

### Working antagonist: Daniel Mercer

Role: senior compliance/records manager for a regional healthcare network.

Objective: protect an earlier fraud involving a vulnerable patient identity that Claire discovered while researching an old family-related record.

The disappearance is intended to make Claire's discovery appear impossible and discredit anyone who reconstructs it.

Important constraint: Daniel is not a mastermind controlling every institution. He understands one records ecosystem extremely well and exploits ordinary human assumptions at the boundaries between systems.

## 3. Why Claire discovered the problem

Months before her disappearance, Claire notices that an old patient/identity record has been corrected repeatedly even though the underlying physical documentation does not support the corrections.

Claire starts comparing old paper records with newer digital records.

She realizes that a small false identity association has been propagated through multiple systems.

She does not know the whole motive, but she understands one key fact:

**once enough systems repeat the same information, later investigators treat the repeated information as independent confirmation even when it originated from the same first error.**

Claire begins collecting evidence.

## 4. Complete evidence timeline — story truth

### T-180 days
Claire notices the first historical discrepancy.

### T-150 days
Claire copies an old document and records the discrepancy privately.

### T-90 days
Daniel realizes someone has noticed the inconsistency.

### T-60 days
Claire begins quietly comparing independent systems.

### T-30 days
Claire contacts Nora after a long period of distance but does not explain everything.

### T-7 days
Claire creates a hidden breadcrumb that Nora will understand only after the disappearance.

### T-1 day
Claire discovers the specific record linking Daniel to the original false identity association.

### Day 0 — disappearance
Claire enters her apartment building.

A legitimate access record later indicates that she leaves at 8:17 p.m.

The record is wrong because a routine access correction is applied to the wrong identity/account.

Security footage shows Claire entering a service corridor but does not show her leaving through the normal route.

Daniel uses the administrative discrepancy to make the official timeline appear coherent.

### Day +1
Police accept the simplest explanation: Claire left voluntarily.

### Day +2
Nora finds the first physical contradiction.

### Day +4
Phone/location information appears to support the official timeline.

### Day +6
Nora discovers that the phone information is not independent proof; it was interpreted through the same identity/account assumption.

### Day +9
A payment/transport record appears to contradict Nora's reconstruction.

### Day +12
Nora discovers that the payment record contains a timestamp generated by a downstream process rather than the physical moment of travel.

### Day +15
A workplace record creates another apparent confirmation.

### Day +18
Nora discovers that the workplace record inherited the same corrected identity information.

### Day +21 — midpoint
Nora finds Claire's breadcrumb and realizes Claire expected someone to reconstruct the timeline after she disappeared.

### Day +24
Nora traces the earliest false entry.

### Day +27
Daniel begins actively pressuring Nora to stop.

### Day +30
Nora discovers the fraud/identity connection.

### Day +33
A trusted supporting character appears to confirm Daniel's story.

### Day +36
Nora realizes the supporting character is repeating a legitimate-looking record rather than independently verifying it.

### Day +39
Nora reconstructs the timeline from physical events outward instead of starting with databases.

### Day +42
She identifies the exact moment when the administrative timeline becomes physically impossible.

### Day +45
Daniel makes one new physical intervention to protect the false timeline.

That action creates the independent physical proof Nora needs.

### Day +46
Nora gives the complete reconstruction to investigators and an independent auditor.

### Day +47
Claire's fate is confirmed.

### Day +50
Daniel's role and objective are exposed.

## 5. Suspect map

### Daniel Mercer — principal antagonist

Motive: protect the original identity/records fraud and prevent Claire from proving the connection.

Means: privileged knowledge of one records ecosystem; influence over corrections and interpretation.

Opportunity: access to processes that propagate identity corrections.

False-alibi mechanism: the administrative timeline appears to place relevant actions elsewhere.

### Ethan Bennett — Nora's estranged brother

Motive: family conflict and money problems.

Means: limited.

Opportunity: plausible but weak.

Function: emotional red herring.

### Mara Lewis — Claire's former supervisor

Motive: fear of professional consequences.

Means: access to some workplace records.

Opportunity: limited.

Function: ambiguous witness who knows part of the truth.

### Victor Hale — building manager

Motive: avoid liability for a security failure.

Means: access to building records and cameras.

Opportunity: strong enough to be suspicious.

Function: procedural red herring and source of the first contradiction.

### Nora Bennett

Not a suspect, but her own guilt and assumptions make her an unreliable interpreter early in the investigation.

## 7. Emotional arc

### Beginning
Nora believes Claire abandoned her emotionally and may have chosen to disappear.

### First transformation
Nora begins to believe Claire was telling the truth about something being wrong.

### Midpoint
Nora realizes Claire trusted her enough to leave a trail specifically for her.

### Crisis
Nora understands that her professional instinct — trusting records — has made her easier to manipulate.

### Final transformation
Nora learns to use records as evidence without allowing records to dictate reality.

### Emotional resolution
Nora does not simply "solve the case." She accepts that her distance from Claire cannot be undone, but she can stop letting the family's old narrative define what Claire meant to her.

## 8. Final reveal test

The ending passes only if the reader can answer all of these questions:

1. What was the first false record?
2. Why was it created?
3. Why did later systems appear to confirm it?
4. Why did investigators miss the dependency between the records?
5. Why did Claire disappear?
6. What did Claire know?
7. What mistake did Nora make early?
8. What physical event finally broke the false timeline?
9. Why could Daniel not simply destroy every record?
10. What evidence remains independently verifiable after the reveal?

If any answer requires a new rule introduced in the final chapters, the outline fails QA and must be revised.

## 9. Target length

Initial commercial target: **80,000–90,000 words**.

Initial architecture target: **36 chapters + short epilogue**.

Average chapter target: approximately 2,100–2,400 words, with shorter chapters permitted around major reversals.

This is a planning target, not a final manuscript word count.

## 10. Chapter architecture pattern

Most chapters should follow:

**Evidence → interpretation → human consequence → decision → new question.**

Cliffhangers should arise from information or consequence, not artificial sentence-level suspense.
