# MARKET OVERVIEW — INITIAL EVIDENCE

Research date: 2026-09-21

## eBook readership

Pew Research Center's October 2025 survey of U.S. adults found 31% had read an e-book in the prior 12 months. By age: 18–29 = 41%, 30–49 = 33%, 50–64 = 26%, 65+ = 23%. Women reported 33% versus men 28%. College graduates reported 42%. These are reading rates, not book purchases. Source: Pew Research Center, April 8–9, 2026.

## Genre signals

A July/August 2026 analysis of 34,700+ U.S. Kindle listings by Pubscout reported:
- Romance: 6,208 listings; 53.3% KDP; 8,023 average reviews.
- Fantasy: 3,422; 62.7% KDP; 9,132 average reviews.
- Self-Help: 2,584; 61.6% KDP; 2,350 average reviews.
- Mystery: 1,716; 46.6% KDP; 2,210 average reviews.
- Thriller: 1,999; 46.3% KDP; 15,762 average reviews.

This measures listings and review depth, not total unit sales.

A separate 2026 Amazon-focused industry analysis identifies mystery/thriller, romance, literature/fiction and science fiction/fantasy among leading digital categories, with self-help and business/money prominent in nonfiction.

## UK signal

NIQ's 2025 UK consumer analysis reports crime & thriller as the leading genre among UK consumers overall and among consumers 45+, while self-help/popular psychology is prominent among men under 45. These findings concern UK book consumers, not exclusively eBook buyers.

## Italy / BookTok signal

TikTok reported, citing NielsenIQ BookData and Media Control, that books driven by #BookTok generated 3.3 million book sales and €41.4 million in Italy in 2025. This is total book sales, not eBooks only.

## Interpretation

The evidence supports researching at least four candidate lanes before choosing:
1. Romance / romantic subgenres
2. Mystery / thriller / suspense
3. Fantasy / romantasy
4. Self-help / popular psychology

No genre is selected yet. Market size alone is insufficient; competition, reader intent, differentiation, series potential and production feasibility must also be tested.
