# P6 SECOND-GENERATION CONCEPT SET — US-FIRST

Date: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

This document creates the second-generation commercial concept set after the first four concepts were stress-tested against current U.S. comparable titles.

The design rule is:

**familiar commercial promise + unusual story mechanism**

The concepts below are hypotheses, not approved book decisions.

## Current market signal

Current U.S. mystery/thriller charts continue to show strong demand for accessible suspense, missing-person investigations, family secrets, and women-centered mysteries. Current examples include Lisa Jewell's *It Could Have Been Her* and Carla Kovach's *Watch Them Disappear*. These signals support the commercial promise of suspense, but they also show why the mechanism must be differentiated rather than relying on a familiar missing-person or family-secret setup.

Current 2026 publishing coverage also shows technology/identity and reality-based mystery mechanisms appearing in the field, including *Before You Were Anne* and other high-concept thrillers. Therefore technology should be used as a story mechanism, not as a novelty label.

## Concept 9 — THE RECORD SAYS SHE NEVER LEFT

**Lane:** psychological / domestic thriller

**Hook:** A woman investigates her sister's disappearance and discovers that every official system says her sister checked out of the building three days after vanishing — but the building's physical security footage proves nobody matching her ever left.

**Mechanism:** institutional records contradict physical evidence; each new verified record creates a new contradiction.

**Promise:** fast investigation, family secrets, escalating proof that the truth has been systematically recorded incorrectly.

**Risk:** overlaps with current records/identity thrillers; must avoid a generic "technology conspiracy" explanation.

**Differentiation requirement:** the contradiction must arise from a realistic chain of ordinary systems, not magical AI.

## Concept 10 — THE WOMAN WHO REMEMBERED TOMORROW

**Lane:** speculative psychological thriller

**Hook:** A crisis counselor receives a frightened caller who accurately describes a murder that has not happened yet — then realizes the caller is describing her own death.

**Mechanism:** information arrives before the event, but only in fragments and with a fixed time limit.

**Promise:** immediate ticking-clock suspense and a clear central question: can the protagonist prevent a crime she has already been told will happen?

**Risk:** future-prediction concepts can become arbitrary.

**Differentiation requirement:** the information must have hard constraints and a credible source within the story world.

## Concept 11 — THE SECOND VERSION OF THE TRUTH

**Lane:** psychological mystery / domestic suspense

**Hook:** After surviving a home invasion, a woman discovers that every witness remembers a different version of the same fifteen minutes — including her husband, the police officer, and the neighbor whose doorbell camera captured the event.

**Mechanism:** multiple independent accounts conflict in specific, testable ways; the protagonist must determine which memories were altered, mistaken, or deliberately manufactured.

**Promise:** puzzle-driven suspense without requiring a missing-person plot.

**Risk:** unreliable-memory territory is familiar.

**Differentiation requirement:** the contradictions must be objectively testable through physical evidence, not resolved only through a final narrator twist.

## Concept 12 — THE DAY THE EVIDENCE CHANGED

**Lane:** mystery / psychological thriller

**Hook:** A forensic photographer notices that crime-scene photographs change after investigators identify a suspect — and the changed images always contain one new detail pointing toward someone else.

**Mechanism:** evidence appears to change in response to interpretation.

**Promise:** high-concept mystery with a simple reader question: who is changing the evidence, and why?

**Risk:** could become supernatural or implausibly technological.

**Differentiation requirement:** the final explanation must remain grounded and fair-play solvable.

## Concept 13 — THE TEN-MINUTE WINDOW

**Lane:** mystery / suspense

**Hook:** A woman discovers that for exactly ten minutes each night, one locked municipal archive becomes accessible under her dead mother's credentials — and every file she opens disappears from the system at midnight.

**Mechanism:** strict time window + disappearing evidence + inherited credential.

**Promise:** repeatable countdown structure with escalating discoveries.

**Risk:** requires a credible reason for the access window and disappearing files.

**Differentiation requirement:** the ten-minute rule must be central to plot architecture, not a gimmick.

## Concept 14 — THE PERSON WHO WAS NEVER REPORTED MISSING

**Lane:** mystery / psychological suspense

**Hook:** A private investigator finds a photograph of a woman in six unrelated missing-person case files — but that woman was never reported missing, and every family whose case she appears in insists they have never met her.

**Mechanism:** one unidentified person appears across unrelated investigations without a corresponding missing-person record.

**Promise:** strong puzzle, episodic discoveries, broad series potential.

**Risk:** could drift into a conventional serial-killer structure.

**Differentiation requirement:** the woman's role must be structurally different from "secret victim" and the resolution must explain the pattern without coincidence overload.

## Concept 15 — THE CALL THAT ONLY SHE RECEIVED

**Lane:** domestic suspense / mystery

**Hook:** During a family emergency, four relatives receive the same emergency call — but each hears a different message, and each is told not to tell the others what they heard.

**Mechanism:** same source, divergent messages, deliberate information asymmetry.

**Promise:** family pressure + puzzle + escalating mistrust.

**Risk:** requires a credible mechanism for differentiated messages.

**Differentiation requirement:** the divergence must be plot-solvable and not depend on a supernatural explanation.

## Concept 16 — THE FILE WITH HER NAME ON IT

**Lane:** psychological thriller

**Hook:** A woman helping clean out a courthouse archive finds a sealed case file dated ten years before she was born — with her full name, current address, and a photograph taken last week.

**Mechanism:** impossible-looking archival evidence tied to a living protagonist.

**Promise:** instant curiosity and identity mystery.

**Risk:** very high plausibility burden; can resemble identity/clone/time-travel fiction.

**Differentiation requirement:** the explanation must be grounded enough for a thriller audience while preserving the central mystery.

## Preliminary screening

No concept is approved yet.

### Strongest mechanisms for next validation

1. **Concept 9 — The Record Says She Never Left**: strongest grounded institutional contradiction.
2. **Concept 14 — The Person Who Was Never Reported Missing**: strongest pure mystery puzzle.
3. **Concept 15 — The Call That Only She Received**: strongest family-information asymmetry.
4. **Concept 13 — The Ten-Minute Window**: strongest repeatable structural device.

Concepts 10, 11, 12 and 16 remain useful but carry higher execution or plausibility risk.

## Validation rule

The next gate is not a subjective ranking. Each candidate must be checked against current U.S. comparable titles for:
- premise overlap;
- mechanism overlap;
- reader-promise clarity;
- title/cover discoverability potential;
- standalone resolution potential;
- series-extension potential;
- plausibility risk;
- twist dependence;
- market saturation.

Only after this evidence pass may one concept be approved for P7 architecture.

## Research basis

Current market checks show that family-centered mysteries remain visible in U.S. publishing, while current suspense charts also include missing-person and disappearance hooks. This makes the commercial promise viable but increases the importance of mechanism-level differentiation. See the current comparable research and cited market sources in the project research log.
