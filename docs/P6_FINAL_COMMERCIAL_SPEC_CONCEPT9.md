## 2. Commercial positioning

Primary market: U.S. adult eBook readers.

Primary lane: psychological mystery / domestic suspense / thriller crossover.

Reader promise:

**A woman searches for her missing sister and discovers that every official record says the sister left — while physical evidence proves she could not have.**

The central buying question is immediate and concrete: **How can the records prove something that physically did not happen?**

## 3. Protagonist

**Working name:** Nora Bennett.

Age: late 30s.

Occupation: insurance-claims investigator / records specialist.

Reason for occupation: she understands documentation, timelines and discrepancies without being a police detective. This keeps the protagonist accessible while making her skill relevant to the mechanism.

Personal weakness: Nora trusts records because records once helped her survive a family crisis. She therefore initially assumes the problem must be her own memory or interpretation.

Emotional wound: she and her younger sister stopped speaking regularly after a family dispute several years earlier. The disappearance therefore carries guilt, not only fear.

## 4. Missing person

**Working name:** Claire Bennett.

Age: early 30s.

Relationship: younger sister.

Claire is not a passive victim. Before disappearing, she had independently discovered an inconsistency in an old family record and had begun tracing it.

This creates a two-level mystery:

1. Where is Claire?
2. What did Claire discover before she vanished?

## 5. Location

Primary setting: a fictional mid-sized American city in the Pacific Northwest.

Reason:
- believable urban infrastructure;
- apartment buildings, hospitals, transit, banks and municipal records can coexist naturally;
- visually distinctive without requiring a famous-city thriller cliché;
- enough rain, winter darkness and geographic isolation for atmosphere without making weather the plot mechanism.

The city should remain fictional so the manuscript does not create unnecessary factual/legal burdens around a real institution.

## 6. Inciting incident

Claire fails to appear for an important appointment.

Nora initially assumes Claire has simply disappeared voluntarily because Claire has recently been distant.

Police identify a building-access record showing Claire left her apartment building at 8:17 p.m.

Nora later obtains a building security image showing Claire entering a service corridor at approximately the same time — but there is no corresponding footage of her leaving.

This is the first contradiction.

## 7. The evidence mechanism

The thriller's signature mechanism is **cross-system contradiction**.

No single record is impossible.

The impossibility appears only when independent systems are placed on the same timeline.

Core systems:

- apartment access log;
- elevator/security footage;
- phone location data;
- rideshare/transport record;
- workplace attendance record;
- payment transaction;
- witness statement;
- building maintenance log.

The antagonist does not need magical access to every system. The false timeline is created through a chain of ordinary actions, assumptions, delayed corrections and selectively manipulated records.

## 8. Antagonist objective

The antagonist's objective is not simply to kill Claire.

The objective is to make Claire's disappearance legally and administratively explainable before anyone can prove what actually happened.

The antagonist therefore wants the official timeline to become stronger than the physical evidence.

This gives the book a distinctive thematic conflict:

**What happens when the record of reality becomes more powerful than reality itself?**

## 9. Midpoint reversal

Nora discovers that the contradiction did not begin on the night Claire disappeared.

A routine record from months earlier contains a small discrepancy involving Claire's identity and location.

Nora realizes someone has been constructing a clean administrative trail around Claire for a long time.

Then she discovers the most damaging fact:

**Claire knew the records were being altered before she disappeared — and she deliberately left Nora one piece of evidence designed to be noticed only after Claire was gone.**

The mystery changes from a missing-person investigation to an investigation of the system being constructed around the disappearance.

## 10. Third-act reveal

The final reveal must not be "the records were hacked."

Instead, the antagonist exploited a chain of legitimate processes:

- a document was entered incorrectly;
- a correction was made using a legitimate request;
- another system copied the corrected information;
- a human assumption filled a gap;
- a witness accepted the official timeline;
- another record then appeared to corroborate the same timeline.

The antagonist deliberately created the first false entry knowing that normal institutional systems would reproduce and reinforce it.

The result is a self-confirming false timeline.

## 11. Final confrontation

Nora does not win by discovering one secret document.

She wins by reconstructing the timeline from the bottom up and demonstrating the exact point where the official timeline became impossible.

The climax should force the antagonist to make one new physical action that cannot be reconciled with the false record.

That action becomes the decisive proof.

## 12. Emotional payoff

The resolution must answer more than "where was Claire?"

Nora must confront her guilt over having accepted the family's version of Claire's behavior before the disappearance.

The emotional resolution is:

**Nora learns that believing a record is not the same as believing a person.**

Claire's fate must be resolved clearly enough for a satisfying standalone ending.

## 13. Standalone ending

The central mystery is fully resolved.

The reader should know:
- what happened to Claire;
- why the false record was created;
- who created it;
- how it survived scrutiny;
- how Nora proved it false;
- what happens to the principal antagonist.

No cliffhanger is required.

## 14. Series extension

Possible series model: Nora Bennett can become an independent investigator specializing in cases where official records conflict with physical evidence.

Future books would use different evidence systems and different human conflicts.

Important: Book 1 must not feel like a pilot episode. It must function completely on its own.

## 15. Cover / blurb positioning hypothesis

The commercial visual should emphasize **documentation versus physical reality**, not a generic missing woman.

Potential visual language:
- security-camera frame;
- timestamp;
- redacted document;
- empty doorway/elevator;
- contradictory times layered over one another.

Avoid generic female silhouette + dark house imagery unless later testing shows a strong reason to use it.

## 16. Main risks

### Risk A — Too technical
Solution: translate every record into a human consequence.

### Risk B — Generic conspiracy
Solution: no omnipotent organization; use a small number of motivated people and ordinary institutional weaknesses.

### Risk C — Implausible data manipulation
Solution: every major contradiction must have a realistic cause that can be explained to the reader.

### Risk D — Passive investigation
Solution: every discovery must force Nora to make a decision, confront someone, travel somewhere, or risk something.

### Risk E — Weak emotional core
Solution: make Nora's relationship with Claire central from chapter one rather than adding emotion after the mystery is built.
