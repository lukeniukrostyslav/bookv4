# P6 FOCUSED DEEP VALIDATION — CONCEPTS 9 AND 15

Date: 2026-09-21

## Gate purpose

This pass stress-tests the two remaining second-generation candidates before any P7 architecture work.

No bestseller, sales, or market-success claim is made here. The purpose is to determine whether either concept has a sufficiently distinct, plausible and repeatable story engine to justify outlining.

## Concept 9 — THE RECORD SAYS SHE NEVER LEFT

### Core commercial promise

A missing-sister investigation in which official records repeatedly prove a version of events that physical evidence disproves.

### Mechanism test

The strongest grounded version does not depend on magical technology or an all-powerful hacker. The contradiction can arise from several ordinary systems:
- building access records;
- CCTV;
- phone location;
- transport/payment records;
- employment or medical records;
- witness statements.

The key is that the systems do not simply contain false data. They are individually plausible and become impossible only when placed on the same timeline.

### Escalation engine

1. The sister disappears.
2. One official record says she left.
3. Physical evidence contradicts it.
4. A second independent system supports the official version.
5. The protagonist finds another physical contradiction.
6. Investigators begin treating the protagonist's interpretation as unreliable.
7. The protagonist discovers that the contradictions predate the disappearance.
8. The mystery changes from "where did she go?" to "who/what caused the record trail to exist?"
9. The protagonist learns that correcting one record creates consequences in another system.
10. The final explanation must resolve the whole timeline without requiring impossible technology.

### Fair-play requirement

The reader must receive enough information to understand the contradictions. The ending cannot be based on a completely hidden technology, secret twin, supernatural intervention or arbitrary identity replacement.

### Emotional engine

The missing sister is not only a puzzle. The protagonist should have a personal reason to distrust the sister or herself, creating a second layer: solving the records also forces the protagonist to confront a family event she previously interpreted incorrectly.

### Major overlap risks

Record manipulation, CCTV contradictions, identity fraud and missing-person investigations are established thriller devices. Current 2026 fiction also demonstrates that manipulated testimony and contradictory evidence can form an entire series engine; for example, *Say That Again* centers on witnesses whose accounts converge on a statement that was never actually spoken. citeturn0search0

Therefore the distinctive element must be the **cross-system contradiction architecture**, not simply "someone manipulated evidence."

### Plausibility scorecard

- Reader clarity: HIGH
- Mechanism plausibility: HIGH if grounded
- Novelty of central mechanism: MEDIUM-HIGH
- Execution difficulty: MEDIUM-HIGH
- Fair-play potential: HIGH
- Emotional stakes: HIGH
- Series potential: MEDIUM-HIGH
- Twist dependence: MEDIUM

These are internal development assessments, not market ratings.

### Decision

**ADVANCE TO FINAL CONCEPT STRESS TEST.**

It is the strongest current candidate, but it is not yet approved.

## Concept 15 — THE CALL THAT ONLY SHE RECEIVED

### Core commercial promise

During a family emergency, four relatives receive different instructions from what appears to be the same source. They are forbidden from telling one another what they heard.

### Mechanism test

The current version has a weakness: "the same call gives everyone a different message" is not automatically credible.

A stronger grounded mechanism is:
- a pre-recorded emergency system;
- each recipient receives a personalized branch based on information known about them;
- the system is designed so recipients believe they are hearing a direct call;
- the messages deliberately conflict.

But this risks becoming a technology/conspiracy thriller unless the family and emergency are compelling enough to carry the story.

### Escalation engine

1. Family emergency begins.
2. Each person receives a different instruction.
3. They follow incompatible instructions.
4. One person breaks the silence and reveals their message.
5. The family realizes the messages were designed to separate them.
6. They attempt to reconstruct the original communication.
7. Every reconstruction reveals another person had information withheld.
8. The family discovers that the sender understood their relationships and fears.
9. The final message forces a choice between protecting one family member and exposing the truth.

### Fair-play requirement

The reader must understand the communications rules early. The plot cannot repeatedly invent new capabilities for the sender.

### Major overlap risks

Different accounts of the same event are a well-established narrative mechanism, and current 2026 material continues to use conflicting witness accounts and memory manipulation. For example, *Say That Again* uses multiple witnesses whose recollections converge in a manipulated way. citeturn0search0

### Core problem

The mechanism is clever, but the reader's first question is likely to become "how did the sender technically do that?" If that question overwhelms "what is happening to this family?", the commercial promise weakens.

### Plausibility scorecard

- Reader clarity: MEDIUM-HIGH
- Mechanism plausibility: MEDIUM
- Novelty of central mechanism: MEDIUM-HIGH
- Execution difficulty: HIGH
- Fair-play potential: MEDIUM-HIGH
- Emotional stakes: HIGH
- Series potential: MEDIUM
- Twist dependence: HIGH

### Decision

**HOLD — DO NOT MOVE TO P7 YET.**

The concept remains useful as a possible alternative, but Concept 9 currently has the cleaner commercial and narrative engine.

# FINAL STRESS TEST FOR CONCEPT 9

## What could make it fail

1. The explanation becomes a generic conspiracy.
2. The protagonist spends too much time reading documents instead of acting.
3. The records are too technical for a broad thriller reader.
4. The final explanation requires an implausible level of institutional corruption.
5. The missing sister becomes only a plot device rather than an emotionally important character.

## Design constraints to prevent failure

- Every major evidence discovery must create an immediate human consequence.
- Technical records should be translated into simple reader-facing contradictions.
- No omnipotent hacker.
- No supernatural explanation.
- No secret twin as the primary solution.
- No unexplained memory wipe.
- At least three major clues must be interpretable before the final reveal.
- The final solution must explain both the physical timeline and the administrative timeline.

## Potential final architecture

**Act I — The Impossible Departure**
The sister disappears. The first official record says she left.

**Act II — The Timeline That Cannot Exist**
Independent records disagree. The protagonist discovers that the contradiction is older than the disappearance.

**Act III — The Person Who Needed the Record**
The investigation reveals why someone needed multiple systems to tell a consistent false story.

**Act IV — Correcting the Record**
The protagonist must decide whether exposing the truth will save her sister, destroy another innocent person's life, or both.

## P6 gate result

**Concept 9 is the only candidate currently recommended for one final approval gate.**

This is not approval for P7 yet. Before outlining, the concept needs a one-page final commercial specification covering:
- definitive protagonist;
- exact missing-person relationship;
- location;
- inciting incident;
- evidence-system mechanism;
- antagonist objective;
- midpoint reversal;
- final reveal;
- emotional payoff;
- title/positioning options;
- standalone ending;
- series extension option.

If that specification survives plausibility and originality review, P6 can move to 80–85% and P7 can begin.
