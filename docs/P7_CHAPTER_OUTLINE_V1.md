# P7 CHAPTER OUTLINE — DRAFT V2

Date: 2026-09-21
Working title: The Record Says She Never Left
Target: 36 chapters + epilogue
Target manuscript: 80,000–90,000 words
Status: revised after P7 continuity QA; requires final QA before freeze

## ACT I — THE IMPOSSIBLE DEPARTURE

### Chapter 1 — The Missing Appointment
Nora waits for Claire to arrive at an important appointment. Claire does not appear. Nora's professional habit of trusting documentation is established through her work.
Turn: Claire's phone is unreachable.

### Chapter 2 — 8:17 PM
Police tell Nora that Claire's apartment-building access history shows an exit at 8:17 p.m.
Turn: Nora remembers Claire's last message placed her elsewhere at approximately that time.

### Chapter 3 — The Service Corridor
Nora obtains security footage. Claire enters a service corridor, but no corresponding physical exit is visible.
Turn: the official exit record has no matching physical event.

### Chapter 4 — The Simple Explanation
Authorities suggest Claire used another route or left voluntarily.
Turn: Nora identifies that every plausible exit route creates another physical contradiction.

### Chapter 5 — Claire's Desk
Nora searches Claire's apartment and finds evidence that Claire had been comparing records.
Turn: an old document contains an unexplained correction.

### Chapter 6 — The First Correction
Nora discovers that the old correction was legitimate-looking but changed identity attribution rather than the underlying physical event.
Turn: Claire had marked the correction as suspicious.

### Chapter 7 — The Decision
Nora decides to reconstruct Claire's final day from physical events first, rather than starting with databases.
Turn: she discovers that the official timeline contains a second apparent confirmation.

## ACT II — THE TIMELINE THAT CANNOT EXIST

### Chapter 8 — The Phone
Phone data appears to place Claire outside the building.
Turn: Nora discovers the raw device event is real, but the person-to-device attribution came from a corrected account/profile.

### Chapter 9 — The Ride
A transport record appears to show Claire traveling across town.
Turn: Nora learns the displayed timestamp is a downstream settlement/ingestion time rather than direct proof of the vehicle's physical position at that moment.

### Chapter 10 — The Workplace
Claire's workplace record appears to corroborate the official timeline.
Turn: the workplace system imported identity information from a source connected to the same original correction.

### Chapter 11 — Three Independent Records
Nora maps the apparent confirmations.
Turn: the records are not truly independent because their identity attribution traces back to one originating correction.

### Chapter 12 — The Family Story
Nora confronts Ethan about Claire's behavior and their family conflict.
Turn: Ethan reveals Claire had recently asked about an old family document.

### Chapter 13 — The Old File
Nora compares an original paper record with later digital records.
Turn: the same identity discrepancy existed months before Claire vanished.

### Chapter 14 — The Supervisor
Nora interviews Mara Lewis. Mara admits Claire was investigating a records anomaly but is afraid to explain further.
Turn: Mara mentions Claire was specifically interested in correction history, not just current records.

### Chapter 15 — The Breadcrumb
Nora finds Claire's printed correction-history page with one timestamp circled and the note: "Ask what was corrected, not what was entered."
Turn: Nora understands Claire expected her to reconstruct the history of an identity association.

### Chapter 16 — The Wrong Question
Nora stops asking "where did Claire go?" and asks "who needed the record to say she went there?"
Turn: she realizes the disappearance may be designed around an administrative explanation.

### Chapter 17 — The Correction Trail
Nora traces correction requests backward.
Turn: multiple later systems inherited the same identity association from one healthcare record.

### Chapter 18 — Midpoint: Claire Knew
Nora proves Claire had identified the propagation problem before disappearing.
Turn: someone with access to the originating record knew Claire was close to the source.

## ACT III — THE FALSE REALITY

### Chapter 19 — Daniel Mercer
Nora meets Daniel, a senior compliance/records manager with legitimate authority over the originating healthcare identity system.
Turn: Daniel gives a reasonable explanation for the correction history.

### Chapter 20 — The Reasonable Story
Nora tests Daniel's explanation against physical evidence.
Turn: one identity attribution cannot be reconciled with the underlying physical event.

### Chapter 21 — The Building Manager
Victor admits that on Day +1 a routine security correction was entered after police requested clarification.
Turn: the correction retroactively links the 8:17 access event to Claire, even though the physical event itself occurred the previous evening.

### Chapter 22 — The Paper Trail
Nora finds the original paper record behind the first false identity association.
Turn: the false association predates Claire's disappearance by months.

### Chapter 23 — The Patient Record
Nora connects the identity discrepancy to an old healthcare record involving another person.
Turn: Claire was not investigating a clerical mistake; she had found evidence that one person's identity had been used to legitimize records belonging to another.

### Chapter 24 — The Missing Link
Nora finds Daniel's role in the originating correction chain.
Turn: motive becomes possible, but Daniel's direct involvement in Claire's disappearance remains unproven.

### Chapter 25 — The Trusted Witness
Mara appears to confirm Daniel's explanation.
Turn: Nora realizes Mara is repeating a legitimate-looking current record rather than independently verifying the historical event.

### Chapter 26 — The Pressure
Daniel warns Nora that continuing could damage Claire's reputation and Nora's professional standing.
Turn: Daniel reveals knowledge of a detail Nora had not made public.

### Chapter 27 — The Physical Timeline
Nora reconstructs Claire's final night from physical events rather than administrative attribution.
Turn: she identifies the precise point where the official timeline becomes physically impossible.

### Chapter 28 — The Motive
Nora identifies the original identity fraud and understands why Claire had to disappear from the official narrative.
Turn: she realizes Claire may still be alive and hidden rather than simply dead.

### Chapter 29 — The Trap
Nora attempts to preserve the evidence through an independent auditor.
Turn: Daniel begins preparing another legitimate-looking correction that would erase the remaining link.

### Chapter 30 — The Last Correction
Nora discovers that one archive item is about to be corrected.
Turn: she designs a test that forces the person responsible for the correction to physically verify the item.

## ACT IV — CORRECTING THE RECORD

### Chapter 31 — The Reconstruction
Nora prepares a complete bottom-up timeline and submits a public-facing records request that Daniel cannot quietly answer without revealing his knowledge.
Turn: Nora predicts Daniel's next physical action.

### Chapter 32 — The Impossible Request
Daniel must personally verify a physical archive item because the electronic record is internally inconsistent.
Turn: Daniel accesses and moves the physical evidence in a monitored location.

### Chapter 33 — The Proof
Independent camera/access evidence captures Daniel's physical intervention.
Turn: his action cannot be reconciled with the official timeline he previously defended.

### Chapter 34 — Claire's Fate
Nora and investigators locate Claire alive but injured and in hiding, having escaped the immediate threat but lacking a safe way to prove what happened.
Turn: Claire confirms the original identity-record discovery and identifies the evidence Nora still needs.

### Chapter 35 — The Record Breaks
Nora, Claire and an independent reviewer reconstruct the complete administrative chain.
Turn: Daniel's role, objective and propagation method are established.

### Chapter 36 — What Really Happened
Daniel's scheme is exposed. Claire's evidence is preserved. Nora accepts responsibility for having believed the family's old narrative about Claire without accepting the false narrative created around the disappearance.
Turn: the central mystery closes without a sequel cliffhanger.

## EPILOGUE — THE RECORD

Weeks later, Nora returns to records work.

A new discrepancy appears.

She does not assume the record is true or false.

She asks what physical event the record is supposed to describe.

Final thematic note: a record is evidence of a claim about reality, not reality itself.
